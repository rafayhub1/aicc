## Quick glossary

- **ERP**: LGU's online student portal system (fees, registration, roll number slips, attendance notifications, results).
- **DSA**: Directorate of Student Affairs.
- **HOD**: Head of Department.
- **VC**: Vice Chancellor.
- **Dean**: Dean of the Faculty.
- **Controller**: Controller of Examinations.
- **GPA**: Grade Point Average of one semester (also called SGPA in scholarship rules).
- **CGPA**: Cumulative Grade Point Average across all semesters.
- **SF**: Grade for failure due to short attendance; treated like F.
- **W**: Grade for an official course withdrawal; not counted in GPA/CGPA.
- **I**: Incomplete grade for a missed exam with an accepted reason; make-up (Grade-I) exam follows.
- **UMC**: Unfair Means Committee (four-member committee formed by the VC).
- **ASA**: Academic Support Assistant (need-based scholars helping faculty, max 5 hours/week).
- **GCR**: Girls' Common Room.
- **Credit hour**: Unit of course workload; 1 theory credit = 1 hour/week; 1 lab credit = about 3 hours/week.
- **Probation**: Academic warning stage after GPA falls below 2.00 while still continuing regular semesters.
- **Re-admission**: Second chance after probations are exhausted; full admission fee applies.
- **Enrollment suspension**: Temporary stop (max one year) of regular semester enrollment after repeated GPA below 2.00.

## Attendance and Leave

### ACP-001: Attendance requirement and leave

**Answer:** You need at least 80% attendance in a course to sit its final exam. LGU has no leave system: every absence (illness, emergency, travel abroad, surgery, coming late, etc.) counts inside the 20% absence limit, so manage your absences carefully.

**Details:**
- Minimum 80% of the classes held in a course are required to appear in that course's final exam.
- There are no leaves at LGU. All absences (emergency, late arrival, travel abroad, surgery/operation or any other genuine reason) must fit within the allowed 20%.
- For a genuine reason, late admission, medical reason or change of course, the teacher may give extra (makeup) classes or material. The Head of Department gets such cases approved by the Dean.
- The Dean of Faculty can relax the limit to 75% in exceptional cases (see ACP-003).
- Students, the HOD and the instructor are informed through the ERP when attendance probation or 'temporary course withheld' applies.

**Students may ask:** "How much attendance do I need?"; "Can I take leave at LGU?"; "What if I miss classes because of sickness or an emergency?"; "How many absences are allowed?"

**Related:** ACP-002, ACP-003, ACP-004

### ACP-002: Attendance probation and 'temporary course withheld'

**Answer:** Before a course is withheld, each course gives you attendance probations as warning stages (two probations for 2, 3 and 4 credit-hour courses; one for a 1 credit-hour course). Once probations are used up and you miss more classes, the course goes into 'temporary course withheld'. You are told through the ERP at each stage.

**Details:**
- Probations per course: 2 for 4-, 3- and 2-credit-hour courses; 1 for a 1-credit-hour course.
- 1st probation begins after this many absences from the start of classes: 4 cr-hr = 2, 3 cr-hr = 2, 2 cr-hr = 1, 1 cr-hr = 1.
- 2nd probation begins after this many more absences once the 1st probation has expired: 4 cr-hr = 2, 3 cr-hr = 2, 2 cr-hr = 2, 1 cr-hr = none (only one probation).
- 'Temporary course withheld' begins after these further absences following the last probation: 4 cr-hr = 4, 3 cr-hr = 2, 2 cr-hr = 1, 1 cr-hr = 1.
- The student, HOD and course instructor are informed via ERP at probation and withheld stages.
- If the withheld-category absence limit is fully used and no special relaxation is granted, a decision on permanent course withheld is made before the final-term exam according to the Vice Chancellor's directions.

**Students may ask:** "What is attendance probation?"; "What does temporary course withheld mean?"; "How many classes can I miss before my course is withheld?"

**Related:** ACP-001, ACP-003

### ACP-003: Short attendance: SF grade and 75% relaxation

**Answer:** If your attendance is below the required level you cannot sit that course's final-term paper and you get an SF grade (failure due to short attendance), which counts like an F. You must repeat the course in a summer or regular semester. The Dean may relax the 80% limit to 75% in exceptional circumstances if the HOD recommends it.

**Details:**
- SF = failure due to short attendance; it is treated like grade F and carries 0.00 grade points.
- The student must repeat the course in the summer semester or a regular semester (subject to the semester credit-hour limit).
- The Dean of Faculty may relax the limit to 75% for a valid reason recommended by the HOD, only in exceptional circumstances, and before the final decision of permanent course withheld.
- The roll number slip shows 'not allowed' against a subject with short attendance (see EXM-003).
- Once an SF or F is awarded you can no longer withdraw from that course.

**Students may ask:** "What happens if my attendance is below 80%?"; "What is the SF grade?"; "Can attendance be relaxed to 75%?"

**Related:** ACP-001, ACP-011, EXM-003


## Semester Freeze, Withdrawal and Enrollment Cancellation

### ACP-004: Freezing (suspending) a semester: who can do it and how

**Answer:** You can freeze a semester before classes begin if you face acute domestic problems or valid difficulties, have passed the previous semester's finals and are still in good academic standing. It needs recommendation from your HOD and Dean and final approval from the Vice Chancellor; the Registrar then issues the notification.

**Details:**
- Apply before the semester classes commence, with a favourable recommendation from the HOD and the Dean of Faculty.
- Eligibility: you must have passed the previous semester's final exams and hold the minimum GPA/CGPA of a good academic student (2.00).
- The freeze is valid only after the Vice Chancellor's approval; the Registrar's office then issues an official notification.
- The first two semesters of a BS cannot normally be frozen. In special hardship (for example iddat, maternity/delivery, death in the immediate family or other justifiable reasons) the competent authority may consider freezing the first semester.
- Fee already paid for a semester that is later frozen is carried forward to the next semester.

**Students may ask:** "Can I freeze my semester?"; "How do I apply for semester freeze?"; "Can I freeze my first semester?"

**Related:** ACP-005, FEE-005

### ACP-005: Rules and effects of a semester freeze

**Answer:** You may not freeze more than two consecutive regular semesters. While frozen you temporarily lose studentship, so hostel, transport, library and medical facilities are not available. The frozen semester still counts toward the maximum time allowed to finish your degree.

**Details:**
- Maximum two consecutive regular semesters can be frozen, within the overall degree-completion timeframe.
- During the freeze you lose studentship temporarily and cannot use university facilities such as hostel, transport, library and medical services.
- A frozen semester counts toward the maximum permissible semesters for the degree.
- After the freeze you can rejoin the same semester or as per the semester enrollment approved by the department; the Vice Chancellor can approve exceptions in special cases.
- Fee paid before the freeze is carried forward to the next semester.

**Students may ask:** "How many semesters can I freeze?"; "Do I lose hostel or library access during a freeze?"; "Does a frozen semester count in my degree duration?"

**Related:** ACP-004

### ACP-006: Withdrawal from a semester

**Answer:** In an emergency or transfer of residence you may withdraw from all courses of a semester before the tenth week of regular classes. It is allowed only once in the whole degree, needs approval up to the Vice Chancellor, and the semester fee is not refunded.

**Details:**
- Apply to your Head of Department with documents and justification before the tenth week from the start of regular semester classes.
- Approval chain: HOD -> Dean of Faculty -> Vice Chancellor; the Registrar's office then notifies the withdrawal.
- All exams and assessments taken in that semester become null and void.
- Maximum one semester withdrawal in the entire degree program.
- The withdrawn semester's courses do not appear on the transcript.
- No refund of the semester fee, whatever the reason.
- The withdrawn semester still counts toward the maximum degree duration.

**Students may ask:** "Can I withdraw from the whole semester?"; "Until when can I withdraw from a semester?"; "Do I get my fee back if I withdraw from the semester?"

**Related:** ACP-007, FEE-005

### ACP-007: Withdrawal from a course (W grade)

**Answer:** You can withdraw from a course with your HOD's permission, normally within 4 to 6 weeks of the start of classes. You get a 'W' on your transcript, which is not counted in GPA/CGPA. You may withdraw from at most 2 courses per semester and have at most 4 W grades in your whole degree.

**Details:**
- Withdrawal is normally allowed within about 4 to 6 weeks from the start of classes, through the departmental ERP coordinator's procedure and with HOD permission.
- Reason accepted: continuing the course would hurt your performance in other courses.
- Limit: 2 course withdrawals per semester and a maximum of 4 W grades in the entire degree.
- Just stopping attending is NOT a withdrawal; you must follow the official process.
- After an 'SF' or 'F' grade has been awarded you can no longer withdraw.
- The W grade appears on the transcript but is excluded from GPA and CGPA.
- A W grade may make you ineligible for certain academic honors/awards.
- The course fee is neither refunded nor carried forward.

**Students may ask:** "Can I drop a course after the semester starts?"; "How many courses can I withdraw from?"; "What is the W grade?"; "Does W affect my GPA?"

**Related:** ACP-011, ACP-021, ADM-008

### ACP-008: Enrollment cancellation and reinstatement

**Answer:** If you are absent for 30 or more days without a documented reason, your enrollment is cancelled automatically. To get it back you must apply for reinstatement, pay 100% of the current admission fee plus any outstanding fee, and it can be done only twice in your whole degree.

**Details:**
- Enrollment is cancelled automatically if a student never joins classes after enrolling, or joins but stays absent, for 30 or more days from commencement without a prior documented reason.
- The HOD sends the cancellation list to the Dean for approval and the Dean sends it to the Registrar office for notification.
- You cannot enroll in a later semester until the absentee period/cancellation is regularized through reinstatement.
- Reinstatement requires payment of 100% of the current admission fee plus outstanding dues.
- Reinstatement is allowed twice in the whole degree program (within the maximum degree duration).
- When enrollment is cancelled, the fee is not refunded or carried forward.

## Grading System and Marks

### ACP-009: How grading works (relative and absolute grading)

**Answer:** LGU uses two systems. Relative grading (based on how the whole class performed) is used when a class has 20 or more students; absolute grading (fixed marks ranges) is used when a class has fewer than 20 students. To pass a course you need at least grade D (1.00 grade point).

**Details:**
- Relative grading: used for classes of 20 or more students. Grades are decided from a histogram of the class's total marks, so your grade depends on your position relative to peers.
- Absolute grading: used for classes of fewer than 20 students, using fixed minimum marks per grade (see ACP-010).
- A student must earn at least 1.00 grade point (grade D) to clear a course.
- Under relative grading the preferable lower limit for a D is 40% marks.

**Students may ask:** "How are grades given at LGU?"; "What is relative grading?"; "What is absolute grading?"; "What is the minimum grade to pass a course?"

**Related:** ACP-010, ACP-011

### ACP-010: Grade points and marks needed for each grade

**Answer:** Grade points: A 4.00, A- 3.70, B+ 3.30, B 3.00, B- 2.70, C+ 2.30, C 2.00, C- 1.70, D 1.00, F and SF 0.00. In absolute grading (classes under 20 students) the minimum marks are: A 85, A- 79, B+ 74, B 70, B- 66, C+ 62, C 58, C- 54, D 50, and below 50 is F.

**Details:**
- Grade points: A = 4.00 | A- = 3.70 | B+ = 3.30 | B = 3.00 | B- = 2.70 | C+ = 2.30 | C = 2.00 | C- = 1.70 | D = 1.00 | F = 0.00 | SF = 0.00.
- Absolute grading minimum marks: A 85 | A- 79 | B+ 74 | B 70 | B- 66 | C+ 62 | C 58 | C- 54 | D 50 | F below 50.
- I = Incomplete and W = Withdrawal carry no grade points.
- In relative grading (classes of 20+), mark ranges are set from the class histogram, so there is no fixed marks table.

**Students may ask:** "What marks do I need for an A?"; "What is the grade point for B+?"; "What is the grading scale?"

**Related:** ACP-009, ACP-014

### ACP-011: Special grades: F, SF, W and I

**Answer:** F means academic failure. SF is failure due to short attendance and counts like F. W means you officially withdrew from the course. I means Incomplete: you missed a mid-term or final for an accepted reason and may take a make-up (Grade-I) exam.

**Details:**
- F: given for academic failure; also when you miss the final-term exam for a reason not accepted by the instructor/HOD/Dean; also when unfair means is proven and approved by the competent authority.
- SF: attendance below the required level, so you are not allowed to appear in the final exam; treated like F (0.00).
- W: successful course withdrawal; shown on transcript, excluded from GPA/CGPA.
- I: awarded when a mid-term/final is missed for an acceptable, approved reason; see ACP-012 for the make-up exam.
- A degree cannot be awarded while any F, SF, I or W remains uncleared in a required course.

**Students may ask:** "What does the F grade mean?"; "When do I get an F or SF?"; "What is grade I?"

**Related:** ACP-012, ACP-021, ACP-022

### ACP-012: Missed exam: Grade-I (make-up) examination

**Answer:** If you miss a mid-term or final for an accepted reason (medical, death in the immediate family, online-exam technical problem, university sports duty or another genuine reason), you can be given Grade I and sit an on-campus or online make-up exam within two weeks of the missed exam. The fee is Rs. 5,000 for one subject or Rs. 10,000 for more than one subject.

**Details:**
- Accepted reasons: (a) medical illness/accident - certificate must be approved by the LGU Medical Officer (not a Government/Military hospital certificate); (b) death of parents, grandparents or siblings - death certificate required; (c) online-exam problems (Moodle login, unstable internet, electricity failure, picture upload, paper submission) approved by the course instructor and ERP representative; (d) representing the university in an approved sports competition; (e) any other genuine reason (for example ISSB) accepted by the instructor and approved by the Dean.
- Your attendance must exceed 80% (or the approved percentage) before the final-term exam.
- The make-up exam is held not later than two weeks from the original date or as per the exam schedule.
- No Grade-I request is accepted for improving marks, and time-barred requests are not entertained.
- Grade-I exams are held in Fall and Spring only, not in the summer semester.
- Process: course instructor recommends -> HOD -> Dean -> Controller of Examinations -> Vice Chancellor. Enrollment for Grade-I is done through the student portal.
- Exam fee charged by the Treasurer's office: Rs. 5,000 if enrolled in one subject; Rs. 10,000 for more than one subject.
- After the Grade-I result the new grade is included in your GPA/CGPA.

**Students may ask:** "I missed my midterm/final exam, what can I do?"; "Which reasons are accepted for a make-up exam?"; "How much is the Grade-I exam fee?"

**Related:** ACP-011, FEE-008

### ACP-013: How your marks are made up (quizzes, mid-term, final, assignments)

**Answer:** A course is assessed through quizzes, assignments/projects/presentations, sessionals, a mid-term (usually 1 hour) and an end-term exam (usually 2 hours). A suggested split for theory courses is quizzes 5-10%, assignments/projects/presentations 5-25%, sessional 10-15%, mid-term 20-25% and end-term 35-40%; the department's Board of Study makes the final decision.

**Details:**
- Theory / partly theory courses (suggested): Quizzes 5-10% (minimum 2 per credit hour; minimum 3 for courses above 2 credit hours) | Assignments/Projects/Presentations 5-25% | Sessional 10-15% | Mid-term (60 min, one) 20-25% | End-term (120 min, one) 35-40%.
- Practical / partly practical courses (suggested): quizzes, assignments/projects and presentations together 30% | Lab work evaluation 30% | End-term exam 40%.
- Courses that are partly theory and partly practical: the final result is the credit-hour-weighted average of the theory and practical percentages (example: Chemistry 3 theory + 1 practical credit hours, theory 75% and practical 65% -> about 72%).
- Psychology case reports: Site Supervisor 15%, Internal Supervisor 35%, External Supervisor 50%.
- In the summer semester an hourly test replaces the mid-term exam.
- Other assessment types that may be used: practical/lab tests, projects, case studies, presentations, online assessments, peer assessment, portfolios, oral exams, online discussion and open-book exams.

**Students may ask:** "How are marks divided in a course?"; "How long are the mid-term and final exams?"; "How many quizzes are there?"

**Related:** ACP-009, EXM-005


## GPA, CGPA and Academic Standing

### ACP-014: How to calculate GPA

**Answer:** Semester GPA = total grade points of the semester divided by the total credit hours attempted that semester, where each course's grade points = grade point of the letter grade x its credit hours. W courses are left out. GPA is shown up to two decimals.

**Details:**
- Formula: GPA = sum of semester grade points / sum of semester attempted credit hours.
- Example: B in a 4 cr-hr course = 12.00; A- in a 3 cr-hr = 11.10; B+ in a 3 cr-hr = 9.90; F in a 2 cr-hr = 0.00; W in a 3 cr-hr is ignored. Total = 33.00 grade points / 12 credit hours = GPA 2.75.
- Rounding: GPA is shown to two decimal places; if the third decimal is 0.5 or more the second digit is raised by one (2.666667 -> 2.67; 2.872223 -> 2.87).
- W is excluded from GPA and CGPA.

**Students may ask:** "How is my GPA calculated?"; "Does a W grade count in GPA?"; "How is GPA rounded?"

**Related:** ACP-015, ACP-010

### ACP-015: How to calculate CGPA

**Answer:** CGPA is your cumulative average: all grade points earned in every semester divided by all credit hours attempted so far. GPA is for one semester only.

**Details:**
- Formula: CGPA = sum of all grade points / sum of all attempted credit hours.
- Example: Semester 1 = 33 grade points over 12 credit hours; Semester 2 = 45 grade points over 15 credit hours; CGPA = (33+45)/(12+15) = 78/27 = 2.89.
- If you repeat a course to improve a D or C-, both grades show on the transcript but the better grade counts in CGPA.
- If you repeat a course with F or SF, only one F/SF is counted regardless of the number of attempts.

**Students may ask:** "How is CGPA calculated?"; "What is the difference between GPA and CGPA?"; "What happens to CGPA when I repeat a course?"

**Related:** ACP-014, ACP-021

### ACP-016: Good academic standing and academic deficiency

**Answer:** You are in good academic standing if your GPA is at least 2.00 at the end of each regular semester and your CGPA is at least 2.00 (applied after enrollment suspension). You are academically deficient if you get any F, a GPA below 2.00, or (after enrollment suspension) a CGPA below 2.00.

**Details:**
- Good standing: GPA >= 2.00 each regular semester and CGPA >= 2.00 (after enrollment suspension).
- Deficiency: (a) grade F in any course, (b) semester GPA below 2.00, or (c) CGPA below 2.00 after enrollment suspension.
- Deficiency is handled through: probation, re-admission, enrollment suspension, separation from the program, and repetition of courses/semester.
- Status after the Fall semester is decided after the Fall result; status after the Spring semester is decided after the Summer result (if a summer semester is offered).

**Students may ask:** "What is good academic standing?"; "When am I academically deficient?"; "When is my academic status decided?"

**Related:** ACP-017, ACP-018, ACP-019, ACP-020, ACP-021

### ACP-017: Academic probation

**Answer:** If your GPA falls below 2.00 for the first time you are placed on 1st probation and still promoted to the next regular semester. A second drop puts you on 2nd probation. A four-year BS allows at most two probations, and both cannot be used in the first four semesters. When your GPA/CGPA returns to 2.00 or above you regain good standing.

**Details:**
- 1st probation: first time GPA < 2.00 in the degree; you are promoted to the next regular semester.
- 2nd probation: GPA < 2.00 again in a later semester.
- Maximum two probations in a four-year bachelor's program; both cannot be availed in the first four semesters.
- Two probations in the first four semesters, or in two consecutive semesters (even after attending summer), makes you eligible to seek re-admission as per department guidance if conditions allow.
- On probation you can take 12 credit hours in the next regular semester.
- GPA/CGPA of 2.00 or above restores good academic status.

**Students may ask:** "What is probation?"; "How many times can I be on probation?"; "How do I get back to good standing?"

**Related:** ACP-016, ACP-018, ADM-004

### ACP-018: Re-admission

**Answer:** Re-admission is a chance given to an academically deficient student after the second probation, at the end of the 3rd or 4th semester, as per department guidance and if conditions allow. You pay the full admission fee and can enroll 12 credit hours (courses with D, C-, F or not yet enrolled).

**Details:**
- After two consecutive probations in the first two semesters: re-admission may be allowed at the end of the third semester; if GPA is still below 2.00 at the end of the fourth semester you are separated from the program.
- After a second probation at the end of the third semester: re-admission may be allowed at the end of the fourth semester; if GPA is below 2.00 at the end of the fifth semester your regular enrollment is suspended.
- If your GPA is 2.00 or above you are promoted to the next regular semester.
- Re-admission requires payment of the full admission fee.
- You can enroll in 12 credit hours (courses with D, C-, F or not-enrolled courses) in a regular semester after department consultation.

**Students may ask:** "What is re-admission?"; "Do I pay a fee for re-admission?"; "How many credit hours can I take after re-admission?"

**Related:** ACP-017, ACP-019, ACP-020

### ACP-019: Enrollment suspension

**Answer:** Enrollment suspension happens when your GPA stays below 2.00 after re-admission or after the second probation at the end of the 4th or a later semester. Regular enrollment is suspended for at most one year (it can be lifted after one or two semesters with the Dean's approval). In this time you can only enroll in courses where you got D, C- or F.

**Details:**
- Triggers: GPA below 2.00 after availing re-admission at the end of the fourth semester, or after second probation at the end of the fourth or later semester.
- Maximum one year; can be removed after one or two semesters with the concerned Dean's approval.
- During suspension you can enroll in subjects with D, C- or F grades, depending on availability.
- If your CGPA is 2.00 or above when suspension ends, you return to regular semesters.
- After suspension you must keep CGPA at 2.00 or above until the degree is completed; if it falls below 2.00 you are separated from the program.

**Students may ask:** "What is enrollment suspension?"; "How long can my enrollment be suspended?"; "What can I study during enrollment suspension?"

**Related:** ACP-018, ACP-020

### ACP-020: Separation (removal) from the program

**Answer:** A student is separated from the program if GPA is below 2.00 at the end of the fourth semester after re-admission, if CGPA is below 2.00 after enrollment suspension ends, on disciplinary grounds, or if the degree cannot be completed within the maximum allowed time. It takes effect after official notification.

**Details:**
- GPA below 2.00 at the end of the fourth semester after re-admission at the end of the third semester.
- CGPA below 2.00 after the enrollment-suspension stage ends.
- Disciplinary grounds under LGU rules.
- Not completing the degree within the maximum stipulated time (including summer); the HOD gets such cases approved by the Dean.
- Separation is effective after notification.

**Students may ask:** "When can I be removed from my degree program?"; "What if I can't finish my degree in the maximum time?"

**Related:** ACP-019, ACP-022

### ACP-021: Repeating courses and improving grades

**Answer:** You can repeat F/SF and W courses to clear them and repeat D or C- courses to improve CGPA, whenever the department offers them (regular or summer semester, within credit-hour limits). Improvement is limited to 5 courses in your whole degree. Repeating courses makes you ineligible for academic honors/awards on the basis of the new grade.

**Details:**
- Clearing F or SF: repeat when the subject is offered. Transcript shows the better attempt and only one F/SF is counted regardless of attempts. You are not eligible for academic honors/awards.
- Clearing W: repeat when offered; transcript shows both grades; not eligible for academic honors/awards.
- Improving D or C-: transcript shows both grades but the better grade counts toward CGPA; maximum 5 courses in the whole degree; not eligible for honors/awards on the basis of the new grade.
- Repeats are arranged by the department in regular or summer semesters, within maximum credit-hour/course limits.
- Alternative courses may be allowed instead of repeating (see ADM-007).

**Students may ask:** "How can I clear an F?"; "Can I improve a D or C- grade?"; "How many courses can I repeat for improvement?"; "Does repeating affect my honors or awards?"

## Degree Requirements and Duration

### ACP-022: Requirements to be awarded a BS degree

**Answer:** To get your degree you need the required credit hours, a CGPA of at least 2.00, no uncleared F, SF, I or W in any required course, and completion of the research project, internship and thesis/case study where applicable. All clearance requirements must also be met.

**Details:**
- Credit hours: old undergraduate policy BS = 124. New undergraduate policy BS: one major = 120; major + one minor = 132; major + two minors = 144; two majors = 198.
- Duration: minimum 4 years, maximum 7 years (HEC limits).
- Minimum CGPA 2.00.
- No uncleared F, SF, I or W grade in any course required for the degree.
- Research project, internship and thesis/case study completed, as applicable to the program.
- A student must meet all clearance requirements.

**Students may ask:** "What do I need to graduate?"; "What is the minimum CGPA for a degree?"; "How many credit hours are needed for a BS?"

**Related:** ACP-023, ADM-014, EXM-020

### ACP-023: Length of semesters and the BS program

**Answer:** Fall and Spring semesters run 16 to 18 weeks including exams. A summer semester is offered once per academic year (about 8 weeks including exams). A BS program is 8 semesters (4 years).

**Details:**
- Fall: 16-18 weeks including exam days. Spring: 16-18 weeks including exam days.
- Summer: one per academic year, 8 weeks including exams (the enrollment policy describes it as 8-9 weeks with double contact hours).
- BS program: 8 semesters (4 years), maximum allowed duration 7 years.

**Students may ask:** "How long is a semester?"; "How many years is a BS?"; "Is there a summer semester every year?"

**Related:** ADM-005, ACP-022

### ACP-024: CGPA below 2.00 in the final semester

**Answer:** If your CGPA is below 2.00 at the end of your final semester you may be allowed to re-enroll in courses where you got below C, together with your upcoming semester, as long as you are not debarred under the CGPA improvement regulation and your program's time limit allows it.

**Details:**
- A minimum CGPA of 2.00 is required for a BS degree.
- Condition 1: you must not be debarred under the CGPA improvement regulation.
- Condition 2: the time duration specified for your program must still allow it.

**Students may ask:** "What if my CGPA is below 2.00 in my last semester?"; "Can I re-enroll to improve my CGPA before graduating?"

**Related:** ACP-021, ACP-022

### ACP-025: Thesis / research project registration and extensions

**Answer:** You can register for the research project/thesis after completing the required prerequisite and corequisite courses. It must be finished within the allotted period. The first extension is free; later extensions cost an extension fee equal to three credit hours each (details from the Treasurer's Office).

**Details:**
- Eligibility: finished the essential corequisite/prerequisite courses and any other prerequisites; registration is mandatory for programs that include it.
- The thesis/project must be completed within the allotted period; after the regular degree duration the department applies for an extension.
- First extension: no additional fee.
- Subsequent extensions: extension fee for three credit hours per extension. Exact details and payment process: Treasurer's Office.

**Students may ask:** "Who can register for thesis or project?"; "Can I get an extension for my thesis?"; "Is there a fee for thesis extension?"

## Grade Review and Honors

### ACP-026: Grade review: how to challenge a grade

**Answer:** First talk to your instructor. If it is not resolved, submit a formal grade review request with evidence to your Head of Department; the Dean can review serious or unresolved cases. Grade review is for errors or unfair process, not for disagreeing with the instructor's academic judgment.

**Details:**
- Valid grounds: calculation errors; grading criteria not applied accurately/consistently; bias or discrimination; procedural errors; inconsistent evaluation compared with similar work; new documentation/evidence (for example overlooked assignments or missing records).
- Step 1: discuss with the instructor. If agreed, the instructor starts a grade-change case.
- Step 2: if unresolved, send a formal request with reasons and evidence to the HOD.
- Step 3: HOD reviews both sides. In extreme or unresolved cases the HOD refers the matter to the Dean, who reviews and decides.
- Step 4: the final decision is communicated to the student; if a change is approved, records are updated.
- The process is confidential and impartial.
- For marks totalling/checking mistakes in an answer sheet see paper rechecking (EXM-013).

**Students may ask:** "I think my grade is wrong, what can I do?"; "How do I request a grade review?"; "On what grounds can I ask for a grade review?"

### ACP-027: Grade change process (after a successful review)

**Answer:** When a review shows the grade should change, the course instructor submits a grade change request with evidence to the Controller of Examinations, who checks it; the Vice Chancellor gives final approval; then it is sent to the Director ERP through the Registrar to update the system.

**Details:**
- Instructor initiates the request with documented evidence.
- Controller of Examinations verifies that the evidence is suitable.
- Vice Chancellor approves and signs the authorization.
- Case is forwarded to the Director ERP via the Registrar so the ERP shows the updated grade.

### ACP-028: Academic medals (gold, silver, bronze)

**Answer:** Gold, silver and bronze medals are awarded at convocation to the top three graduates (highest, second and third degree CGPA) of a session. All need a CGPA of at least 3.50, degree finished in the minimum 4 years, every course cleared at the first attempt (no repeats, F grades, withdrawals or semester freeze), no transferred credits and no disciplinary punishment.

**Details:**
- Gold: highest degree CGPA (>= 3.50); session needs at least 10 students. Silver: second-highest CGPA (>= 3.50); session of at least 10 students. Bronze: third-highest CGPA (>= 3.50); session of at least 50 students.

## Disciplinary Procedure

### DSC-003: Disciplinary Committee and how a case is handled

**Answer:** The University Disciplinary Committee, chaired by the Director Student Affairs with eight members nominated by the Vice Chancellor, handles misconduct. The process is: written complaint to the Directorate of Student Affairs, preliminary review, written notice to the student, hearing where you can present your defense, decision, written communication through the Registrar, and appeal to the Vice Chancellor within 7 working days (final).

**Details:**
- Committee: Director Student Affairs (President) + eight members from academic and administrative departments nominated by the VC.
- Step 1 - Reporting: anyone (faculty, staff, student) can report; complaints must be written and sent to the Directorate of Student Affairs.
- Step 2 - Preliminary review: the Director assesses; minor issues may be solved via counseling, warning or mediation; serious cases go to the Committee.
- Step 3 - Notice: the accused gets a written notice with the allegation, date/time/venue of hearing and the right to present defense, witnesses or evidence.
- Step 4 - Hearing: formal hearing; the student can present their case, call witnesses and submit documents; both sides are questioned.
- Step 5 - Decision: findings recorded; minor penalties can be applied by the Committee; major penalties (suspension, expulsion) need the Vice Chancellor's approval.
- Step 6 - Outcome: communicated in writing through the Registrar's Office and a copy goes into your official record.
- Step 7 - Appeal: within 7 working days of notification to the Vice Chancellor, whose decision is final.
- The process is meant to be corrective rather than punitive; confidentiality, fairness and impartiality are maintained.

### DSC-007: Anti-harassment policy: what it covers

**Answer:** LGU does not tolerate harassment or victimization. Harassment is unwanted conduct that violates someone's dignity or creates an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating or offensive environment, whether in person, in writing or electronically, directly or through a third party. It includes bullying, stalking, sexual harassment and abusing authority for grades or favours, and a one-off incident can count. It is punishable under LGU policy, the Pakistan Penal Code, the Punjab Protection against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act 2010 and the HEC Sexual Harassment Policy.

**Details:**
- Covers unwanted, discourteous, hostile, ridiculing, degrading or abusive comments, remarks and actions; a single incident can be harassment.
- Forms: face-to-face, written or electronic/mobile communication, direct or to a third party, and a culture that tolerates harassment or bullying (for example offensive jokes).
- Stalking: repeated unwanted behavior such as following, contacting, monitoring online activity, loitering, interfering with property, or spying including with CCTV/electronic surveillance.
- Sexual harassment: unwanted sexual advances, requests for sexual favours, sexually demeaning communication or conduct, including misuse of authority such as promising better grades in return for favours, asking students to meet outside campus or in offices after hours, or blackmail with fake documents/pictures.
- Victimization (punishing someone for complaining, supporting a complaint or taking part in an investigation) is also prohibited.
- Being intoxicated is not an excuse. Respectful academic debate is not harassment.
- Intimidation or character assassination of faculty/staff by students or colleagues is also covered.
- Legal basis: LGU Policy and Procedure on Harassment, Pakistan Penal Code, Punjab Protection against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act 2010, HEC Sexual Harassment Policy.

## Credit Hours and Courses

### ADM-001: What a credit hour is and how course codes like 3(3+0) work

**Answer:** A credit hour measures the workload of a course. For theory courses 1 credit hour = 1 hour of teaching per week for the semester; for lab/practical/project work 1 credit hour = 3 hours of lab or practical work per week. Notation like 4(3+1) means 4 credit hours total: 3 theory and 1 lab/practical.

**Details:**
- Credit hour = total workload including contact time and expected study effort outside class.
- Theory: 1 credit hour = 1 hour per week throughout the semester (a 3-credit course can be 3 x 1-hour classes, 2 x 1.5-hour classes or 1 x 3-hour class).
- Lab/practical/project: 1 credit hour = about 3 hours of lab contact per week.
- 3(3+0): 3 credit hours, all theory. 4(3+1): 4 credit hours, 3 theory + 1 lab/studio/field/practical. 4(1+3): 4 credit hours, 1 theory + 3 practical.
- The first number is the theory component and the second the practical component; a hyphen may be used instead of a plus sign.

- General education: 30 credit hours; major courses: 30 to 42 credit hours.
- Internship/field experience: 6-8 weeks, 3 credit hours, graded by faculty with the field supervisor (exemption possible where HEC/NCRC or a professional council already prescribes an internship).
- Minimum CGPA 2.00 out of 4.00.

**Students may ask:** "What is an Associate Degree?"; "How long is the Associate Degree?"; "How many credit hours are in an AD?"

**Related:** ADM-018

### ADM-018: Entry to BS after an Associate Degree and exit with an Associate Degree

**Answer:** Associate Degree holders can join the 5th semester of a BS in the same discipline without deficiency courses, or after a bridging semester if the discipline differs, with a minimum 2.00 CGPA (LGU may set higher). BS students can exit with an Associate Degree after at least 60 credit hours (30 of them general education) over 4 semesters, CGPA 2.00 or above, the same subject field, and statutory body approval, in accredited disciplines.

**Details:**
- AD to BS: admission into the 5th semester of an undergraduate degree in the same discipline without deficiency courses.
- Different discipline: deficiency courses through a bridging semester before the 5th semester.
- Minimum eligibility for 5th-semester admission: 2.00/4.00 CGPA in the AD; the university may set higher criteria.
- Exit with AD: at least 60 credit hours over at least four semesters, including 30 general education credit hours; CGPA at least 2.00; the degree subject field must remain the same; approval of the concerned statutory body; allowed only in disciplines accredited by councils such as NCEAC, NBEAC and NACTE.
- Courses exempted for higher degree are decided by the admitting university case by case.

**Students may ask:** "Can I go from an Associate Degree to BS?"; "Can I leave my BS with an Associate Degree?"

**Related:** ADM-017
